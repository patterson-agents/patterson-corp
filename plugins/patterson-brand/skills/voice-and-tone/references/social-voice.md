# Social media voice — full reference

Sources: **Patterson Companies Social Media Writing Guidelines**, 09/2019 `[SOC19]`, and **Brand Guide
2025** pages 47–49 `[BG25]`.

---

> [!NOTE]
> The ✅ and ❌ marks below reproduce Patterson's own published correct/incorrect examples. They are
> quoted content, not decoration.

## 1. Scope

> "In general, the same writing guidelines outlined in the Brand Voice section should be followed on
> social media. Clear and concise, our brand voice on social media should be **positive, authentic and
> professional**."

Social is a **layer on top of** the corporate voice, not a replacement for it.

## 2. The key difference

> "The main difference between writing for social media versus writing for owned media such as our
> websites or print publications is that **the audience may not necessarily be in a buying mindset**…
> Social media copy should **limit sales-driven language** and instead focus on **educating, informing
> and entertaining**."

> [!IMPORTANT]
> `[BG25 p.47]` **80/20 rule:** 80% of content should provide value; at most 20% may be promotional.

## 3. Point of view — non-negotiable `[SOC19]`

> "We are speaking on behalf of Patterson as a whole. Therefore, we **always write in first-person
> plural: We, us, our/ours** — not I or they."

| | Example |
|---|---|
| ✅ | *We had a great afternoon welcoming 12 new territory representatives from around the United States to our corporate headquarters in Minnesota this week!* |
| ❌ third person | *This week, Patterson welcomed 12 new territory representatives… to **their** corporate headquarters… **They** are a company who truly prides **themselves**…* |
| ❌ first person singular | ***I** had the pleasure of welcoming 12 new territory representatives… **I** can't wait to introduce them…* |

## 4. Guiding principle `[SOC19]`

> "A good rule of thumb when creating social copy is to conduct yourself the same way you would as the
> polite host of a social gathering."

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Ask about things you know matter to them | Talk about yourself too much or hog the conversation |
| Use approachable language that is conversational and positive | Be boastful — share what you're proud of tastefully |
| Conduct yourself with dignity at all times to avoid regrets | Leave anyone out — welcome all the other party guests, especially those new to your friend circle |

## 5. Hashtags `[SOC19]`

- Research every hashtag: confirm it is used frequently, means what you think it means, and is not too
  vague. **Check every hashtag on every platform it will be used on.**
- **Capitalize the first letter of each word** for readability: `#ThisIsAnExample`, not
  `#thisasanexampleitalljumblestogether`.

| Platform | Count | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Facebook | Emphasis only | Hashtags are hyperlinked and discoverable, but strict user privacy settings limit their reach |
| X (Twitter), LinkedIn | **Two or three** per post | — |
| Instagram | **Up to 15** per post | Used for discovery |

> [!WARNING]
> **Avoid `#Patterson`** — a search returns towns named Patterson, athletes named Patterson and
> unrelated posts.

| | Example |
|---|---|
| ✅ | `#DidYouKnow: 1 in 4 dentists list hemostasis among their top 3 challenges. See how Hemoban Gel simplifies tissue management and can prevent secondary bleeding. https://patt.dental/newproducts #OralHealth` |
| ❌ | `#DidYouKnow: 1 in 4 #dentists list #hemostasis among their top 3 challenges… #tissuemanagement… #bleeding… #Patterson` |

Why the second fails, per `[SOC19]`: too many hashtags reduce readability; `#tissuemanagement` is used
too infrequently to be worth including; `#bleeding` and `#Patterson` are both too vague.

## 6. Style on social `[SOC19]`

- Special characters — `!`, `?!`, `***`, emoji and all caps — **enhance, do not overpower** a post.
- Most platforms allow no formatting beyond spaces and line breaks; bold, italic, underline and
  strikeout are not possible.

## 7. Channel strategy `[BG25 p.47]`

Active channels: **Facebook, LinkedIn, Instagram, X (Twitter), YouTube.**

- **Be active.** Post regularly and respond to questions and comments. *"An inactive social media
  account is off-putting to anyone visiting the page and should be taken down entirely."*
- **Provide engaging content** — relevant, informative and authentic.
- **Focus on stories.** Content that tells a story and informs is more interesting than images and
  messaging that push products or services.
- **Use strong images** focused on real people, places and things. Graphic- and text-heavy images
  usually perform poorly compared with photos featuring people.

Social also serves reputation management and crisis communication, customer insight, service and
support, targeted advertising and SEO.

Contact: `corporatemarketing@pattersoncompanies.com`.

## 8. Imagery `[BG25 p.48]`

| Content | Format |
|---|---|
| Graphics | **.png** — social sites compress; PNG loses the least data |
| Photos without graphics or text overlays | **.jpg** |

Create **two** graphics for most campaigns: **1200 × 628 (1.91:1)** and **1080 × 1080 (1:1)**.

| Instagram surface | Size |
|---|---|
| Square | 1080 × 1080 |
| Portrait (4:5) | 1080 × 1350 — most feed space, but crops to square on the profile grid |
| Landscape | 1080 × 608 |
| Stories | 1080 × 1920 |

Video must be **.mp4 or .mov** (no animated GIFs), **30fps**, **≥ 3 seconds**. Vertical preferred.

> [!TIP]
> Images featuring people in a real-life setting perform best — particularly on Facebook, whose feed
> algorithm weights people over products or text/graphic-heavy content.

## 9. Favicon and profile-image changes `[BG25 p.49]`

The hands-only favicon may be recolored on social **only**, by corporate marketing, tied to a
pre-agreed **month-long observance**, at most **twice per year**, never for one-off events and never
for customer marketing campaigns. Documented examples: a Facebook header for Black History Month and a
LinkedIn header for Pride Month.
