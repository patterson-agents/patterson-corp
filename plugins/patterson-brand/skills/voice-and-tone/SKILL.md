# Patterson voice and tone

**The voices are additive, not alternatives.** The corporate voice `[BG25 p.10–12]` applies
everywhere. Patterson Dental marketing copy layers `[VOICE18]` **on top** of it. Social layers
`[SOC19]` on top.

---

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Never blend the layers into one generic voice, and never substitute a sub-brand voice for the
> corporate one. **Establish which voice applies before writing.** If the audience or channel is
> unclear, ask.

> [!NOTE]
> The ✅ and ❌ marks in this skill reproduce Patterson's own published correct/incorrect examples
> from the Brand Guide and the Social Media Writing Guidelines. They are quoted content, not
> decoration — Patterson's guidance permits emoji in social copy where they "enhance, do not
> overpower". No other skill in this plugin uses them.

## 1. Corporate voice — always in force `[BG25 p.10–12]`

> "At our core, we communicate in a positive, customer-first style that is **uncluttered, friendly and
> authentic**."

Four attributes: **Optimistic · Clear · Truthful · Personable.**

| Attribute | What it means | Published example |
|---|---|---|
| **Optimistic** | Rewrite negatives as positives; focus on solutions, not problems | ✅ *Rest assured your SDS requirements are covered.*<br>❌ *What keeps you up at night?* |
| **Clear** | Declutter. Remove unnecessary prepositional phrases, "ands" and "thats". Avoid *great, special, really, very*. **One thought per sentence.** Find the right word instead of using an adverb | — |
| **Truthful** | Write authentically, avoid internal jargon, focus on how customers feel. We communicate **with** the customer, not **at** them | — |
| **Personable** | Ask: does it sound like a caring person wrote it? Is the level of formality right? | — |

Baseline `[BG25 p.11]`: **AP style** (AMA for medical articles), **active voice**.

Digital writing `[BG25 p.11–12]`: use the words customers use; people skim, so make content
scannable; headlines and subheads must communicate the intent of the content.

## 2. Patterson Dental marketing voice `[VOICE18]`

Explicitly *"an addendum to the Patterson Brand Guide"* — it **adds to** §1, it does not replace it.

> "If our copy is empathetic, empowering and authoritative, we will always be on brand."

| Attribute | What it means | Guardrail |
|---|---|---|
| **Empathetic** | We do not just understand what the customer is talking about — we have the insider knowledge to feel what they feel. Written for doctors, office managers, hygienists and assistants | Emotional connection = good. **Overselling = bad.** |
| **Empowering** | Every piece should make the customer think about the next step toward their vision. Aspirational language grounded in business facts | **Nudge, do not shove**, toward purchases |
| **Authoritative** | The professionals we address are experts; we are experts on the business practices, equipment, technology, software and merchandise a dental team needs | *"There is no reason to be shy."* |

Positioning:

> "Patterson Dental is relied upon for expertise and is trusted to support dental practices around the
> country as they endeavor to grow both in size and capacity for care."

> [!WARNING]
> `[VOICE18]` cites "140+ years in business" as written in 2018. **Verify the current figure before
> reusing it.**

## 3. Social media voice `[SOC19]`, `[BG25 p.47–49]`

Same corporate guidelines, but **positive, authentic and professional**, with three hard differences:

1. **The audience may not be in a buying mindset.** Limit sales-driven language; focus on
   **educating, informing and entertaining**. `[BG25 p.47]` sets an **80/20 rule** — 80% of content
   provides value, at most 20% is promotional.
2. **First-person plural, always.** *We, us, our/ours.* **Never *I*. Never *they*.** We speak on
   behalf of Patterson as a whole `[SOC19]`.
3. **Behave like the polite host of a social gathering** — ask about what matters to them, use
   conversational and positive language, keep your dignity, and welcome everyone. Do not hog the
   conversation or be boastful.

Hashtags:

| Platform | Count |
|---|---|
| X (Twitter), LinkedIn | Two or three |
| Instagram | Up to 15 |
| Facebook | Emphasis only |

Capitalize the first letter of each word (`#DidYouKnow`). Research every hashtag on every platform.
**Avoid `#Patterson`** — too vague, it returns towns and athletes.

> [!TIP]
> Special characters, emoji and all caps **enhance, never overpower**, a post `[SOC19]`.

## 4. Internal communications `[BG25 p.43–46]`

> "Internal communications need to follow the same Patterson standards of voice, style and font
> selection."

**Patterson Priorities** is the weekly all-employee newsletter, sent every Thursday. Articles run
**about 300 words**, cover who/what/where/when/how, include a visual where available, and are due to
Corporate Communications by **the Friday before publication**.

## 5. Email `[BG25 p.28–32, p.58]`

Hero headlines, subject lines, preheader text and subheads are **all sentence case**. Email signatures
project the brand, not personal style: no photo, no multiple colors or fonts, no inspirational quote,
no social icons, no ads. Secondary signature graphics are **informational, not promotional**, and
static.

## 6. Rewriting rules

When asked to make copy "on brand":

1. Fix case first — sentence case is the most common violation.
2. Cut adverbs, intensifiers and jargon; split multi-thought sentences.
3. Convert passive to active.
4. Reduce CTAs to one (never more than three).
5. Check point of view — first-person plural on social.
6. Preserve the sub-brand layer. Dental copy must stay empathetic/empowering/authoritative; do not
   flatten it into generic corporate copy.

> [!CAUTION]
> Never invent a claim, statistic or year-count. If the draft cites one you cannot source, flag it.
