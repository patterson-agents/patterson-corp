# Editorial mechanics — full reference

Source: **Patterson Companies Copy Style Guide, 04/2022** `[COPY22]` unless noted. Base references are
the AP Stylebook (2022) and Webster's New World College Dictionary. `[BG25 p.N]` = Brand Guide 2025.

---

## 1. Case

| Context | Case | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Headlines | Sentence case, always | `[BG25 p.59]` |
| Subheads, secondary subheads | Sentence case | `[BG25 p.59]` |
| Body copy, captions | Sentence case | `[BG25 p.59]` |
| Buttons / CTA | Sentence case, **never all caps** | `[BG25 p.57]` |
| Eyebrows, text links | Sentence case | `[DS20 p.10]` |
| Email subject lines, preheader text | Sentence case | `[BG25 p.58]` |
| Titles (print only) | May be all caps | `[BG25 p.25]` |
| Titles, footer text | Extrabold or Semibold, all caps | `[BG25 p.27]` |
| Email signature promise line | Arial Bold, all caps | `[BG25 p.28]` |
| Hashtags | Capitalize the first letter of each word | `[SOC19]` |

> `[BG25 p.26]`: "Since all-caps is regarded as shouting in the digital space, we need to be aware of
> decorum, as well as the use of audible reading technology for some users… all-caps in headlines may
> no longer be practical. A mix of primarily sentence case and occasional all-caps in limited areas
> can work."

> [!WARNING]
> **Superseded:** the 2022 guide's *"Titles should be all caps."* Do not follow it.

## 2. Exceptions to AP style

| Item | Patterson rule | AP would say |
|---|---|---|
| Academic degrees | **No periods:** BA, PhD, RDH, DDS, DVM, CPA | with periods |
| Brackets | Use **brackets** to clarify within quoted material | parentheses |
| Cents | Dollar sign and decimal: `$.06`, `$.50` | cent sign (¢) |
| Italics | Italics for book/journal/magazine/newspaper titles and scientific names (*Staphylococcus aureus*) | no italics |
| Serial comma | **No Oxford comma** in a simple series (*red, white and blue*); **do** use it in a complex series for clarity. Doctor-authored articles following AMA style may use it | — |

## 3. Numbers

- Spell out zero through nine; use numerals for 10 and above — **except** when a number begins a
  sentence.
- Always numerals for ages, dates, dimensions, percentages, measures and weights.
- Fractions less than one in body copy: spell out and hyphenate — *one-third*, *three-fourths*.
- Decimals under 1 take a leading zero: *increased 0.9 percent*.
- **Percent: use the symbol (%) in copy.**
- Millions and billions: `$1M`, `$1B`. `K` only for money in tight space (*Median Income Is $55K*);
  elsewhere `K` means kilometre (*a 5K race*).
- Dollars: figures with `$`. Financial communications include cents (*$0.51*); marketing removes
  `.00` (*$100*, not *$100.00*). Never *$1 billion dollars*.

## 4. Dates and time

- *January 1, 2015*. Comma after the weekday: *Wednesday, July 22, 2015*.
- **No month or day abbreviations.** No *-st*, *-nd*, *-rd*, *-th*.
- Numerals-only grids use a four-digit year: *1/1/2015*.
- Hyphen for inclusive academic years: *the 2006-07 academic year*.
- Never use a hyphen as a substitute for *to* or *and* after *from* or *between*.
- **En dash ( – ) with a space on both sides**, not a hyphen: *6:30 – 7 p.m.*
- Time: omit `:00` on the hour (*6 p.m.*). Lowercase with periods and a space (*6:30 p.m.*).
  Stylised, designed copy may occasionally use *AM, PM*.
- Fiscal year: lowercase spelled out (*fiscal 2017*); no space abbreviated (*FY17*).

## 5. Phone, web and email

- **Phone numbers use periods, not hyphens:** `505.884.6747`. No leading "1" on toll-free numbers.
  Comma before an extension: `866.234.2165, ext. 61727`.
- No `http://` in copy. `www.` may be removed — always confirm the shortened address still links.
- Write addresses lowercase: `pattersondental.com`, `your.name@pattersoncompanies.com`.
- Long URLs get visual direction: `Pattersonvet.com > Equipment > Anesthesia > Versa II`.
- Site directions: **italicize directions, use angle brackets ( > ), bold action words**
  `[BG25 p.10]`.

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Correct** | *Learn more at pattersonvet.com > equipment > finance > Get Prequalified* |
| **Wrong** | *To learn more, go to pattersonvet.com, then click "Equipment" then click "Finance"…* |

## 6. Punctuation and mechanics

- One space after a period.
- Colon before a list **only after a complete sentence**.
- Asterisk links to fine print — never used purely for emphasis. In body copy asterisks go **after**
  punctuation; in a footnote, no space between the asterisk and the copy.
- Exclamation points: **use sparingly**.
- Quotation marks: doubles by default; singles only for a quote within a quote. Periods and commas
  inside; semicolons, dashes etc. outside.
- Apostrophes in plurals: *p's and q's*, but *ABCs*, *the 1970s*. Decades take a right single
  quotation character: *the '60s*.
- `Q&A`, not `Q & A`. No spaces around ampersands in such constructions.
- Ampersands are generally not a substitute for *and*. Permitted in catalog page/section headers
  (*Finishing & Polishing*), trademarked vendor names (*Johnson & Johnson*), and PowerPoint titles or
  bullets to save space — be consistent within the deck.

## 7. Capitalization

- Proper nouns and trademarked names capitalized; lowercase the generic term that follows (*Porter
  flowmeter*).
- Business units and department names capitalized, but **not** the words *department*, *team*, etc.:
  *the Finance department*, *the Companion Animal division*, *the Human Resources team*.
- Job titles — including C-level — capitalized **before** a name only: *Vice President of Sales Jane
  Doe* / *Jane Doe is vice president of sales*.
- Copy titles: initial caps except conjunctions, articles and prepositions; prepositions of five or
  more letters are capped (*Through*).
- *company* lowercase as a short reference to Patterson.
- **Corporate Office** (not General Office), initial capped, for the Patterson headquarters.
- **Branch** capitalized with a specific location (*Patterson Dental Boise Branch*), lowercase
  generally.
- Directions: lowercase as compass points, capitalized as regions (*the West Coast*, *the Midwest*).
- Diseases lowercase except proper-noun portions: *arthritis*, *swine flu*, *Down syndrome*, *Lyme
  disease*.
- *Black* capitalized in racial, ethnic or cultural references.
- Seasons lowercase unless part of a proper name.
- *doctor*, *veterinarian* lowercase in general references.
- **Presidents Day** (no apostrophe). **Social Security number** (US) / **Social Insurance Number
  (SIN)** (Canada). **ZIP code**.

## 8. Names, credentials and titles

- Full name on first reference, last name only after. New-hire and bio copy may use the first name on
  subsequent references.
- **Never** *Dr.* together with a degree abbreviation: *Dr. Janette Pinedo* **or** *Janette Pinedo,
  DDS* — not both.
- Spelled-out credentials in general body copy are lowercase (*He is a certified public accountant*).
- Areas of study lowercase unless a proper noun.
- Initials take periods and no space: `M.F. Patterson`.
- `Jr.`, `Sr.`: no comma before.
- Fellowship abbreviations separated by commas: `Howard S. Glazer, DDS, FAGD, FACD, FICD, FASDA,
  FAAFS`.
- C-suite: *CEO* acceptable in all references; *chief financial officer* / *chief operating officer*
  spelled out on first reference then CFO/COO; all other C-level titles spelled out.
- Photo captions: `From left: (front) Title Name, Title Name, Title Name; (back) Title Name, …`

## 9. Units and measures

- Standard SI units: `g` (not gm) for gram.
- **Space before the unit:** `5 ml syringe`, not `5ml syringe`.
- Millilitre may be `mL` or `ml` by business unit — **use `mL` for Veterinary**.

## 10. UI copy `[DS20 p.10]`, `[BG25 p.59]`

> "Consistency in the way we call users to action and display recurring types of information allows
> for scannable content."

- Sentence case for headlines, buttons, text links and eyebrows.
- **No punctuation in buttons.**
- **No phone numbers in buttons** — phone numbers use text-link styling with the "phone" media icon.
- CTAs must be intentional and actionable, and **never written as questions**.
- Link text for an email address must not display the address itself.
- Eyebrows are short, clear, concise, sentence case, and typically reflect the name of their
  sub-category. **Eyebrows are not headlines** `[DS20 p.16]`.

> [!IMPORTANT]
> Alerts must use the dedicated alert pattern and the correct state. *"If the user did not do
> something wrong, do not use the 'error' message box"* `[DS20 p.37]`.

## 11. Brand promise `[BG25 p.6, p.28]`

| Situation | Correct form |
|---|---|
| Running copy | *Trusted Expertise, Unrivaled Support* — **comma, no registration mark** |
| Designed graphic | **Trusted Expertise. Unrivaled Support.®** — periods, small superscript ® |
| Frequency | Once per communications piece |
| As a headline | Only in broad pieces focused on the whole company |
| Placement | A visual element; a sign-off on the back of print materials |

| | Example |
|---|---|
| **Wrong** | *At Patterson, we live up to our promise of "Trusted Expertise. Unrivaled Support." every day.* |
| **Correct** | *At Patterson, we live up to our promise of Trusted Expertise, Unrivaled Support every day.* |

> [!CAUTION]
> The mark changed from ™ to **®** in 2023. Legal monitors the marketplace for misuse.
